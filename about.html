A career without a straight line.

Healthcare, entrepreneurship, public service, technology and research are different perspectives on the same goal: making systems work better.

WHO I AM

A career without a straight line.

I did not arrive at research through a traditional academic route. My professional life started in emergency response, where decisions had to be made quickly, communication had to be clear and responsibility was immediate. That environment shaped the way I still think about organisations: systems matter, but people, judgement and coordination matter just as much.

Later I built and ran my own transport business. It taught me a different form of responsibility: customers, cash flow, operations, service quality and the consequences of everyday decisions. After that I moved into public service, including Community Payback supervision and placement coordination.

DIFFERENT ENVIRONMENTS, ONE PERSPECTIVE

Practice came before theory.

Emergency response, entrepreneurship and public service look very different on paper. In practice, each taught me something about leadership, risk, people, systems and the gap between a plan and what actually happens.

WHY I KEEP LEARNING

Learning became the way I connect the pieces.

Returning to higher education gave me a way to organise experience rather than simply collect it. Cloud Computing developed my understanding of technology. The MBA helped me connect technology with strategy, operations and organisational decision-making.

The next step is research. I am not trying to replace practical experience with theory. I want to use research to examine it more carefully, challenge assumptions and build evidence that can be useful beyond my own experience.

“From Experience to Evidence” describes the direction I am taking.
